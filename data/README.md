# Synthetic store dataset

`store_synth.csv` — one row per item per day, 3 years (2023–2025), 9 prepared-food
items. Generated by `python -m sim.generate`; every parameter lives in `sim/params.py`.

**No Harris Teeter (or any real) data was used to build this.** The dataset exists to
prove the *method* — that demand with realistic structure is learnable and that
forecast-driven production beats par-sheet production — not to estimate any real
store's numbers.

## Columns a real store could observe

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `date`, `item`, `item_name`, `dept`, `dow` | identifiers; `dow` 0=Mon |
| `holiday` | named holiday or empty |
| `is_closed` | store closed (Christmas) |
| `tmax_f`, `weather` | daily high °F; sunny/cloudy/rain/snow |
| `snow_tomorrow` | tomorrow's snow forecast, known today |
| `payday` | day 1–3 or 15–17 of month |
| `produced`, `sold`, `wasted` | units; `wasted = produced − sold` (day-fresh) |
| `stockout` | sold out (sold ≥ produced) — observable sellout flag |
| `unit_price`, `unit_cost` | retail price, cost of goods |

## Simulation-only columns (scoring, never training)

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `true_demand` | what customers would have bought with unlimited supply |
| `lost_sales` | `true_demand − sold` — invisible in a real store |
| `true_mean` | latent expected demand before noise (used by the oracle policy) |

Models train **only on observable columns**; on sellout days sales is treated as a
*lower bound* of demand (censored loss). The simulation-only columns are used solely
to score policies afterward.

## Built-in structure (what a model can learn)

Day-of-week profiles per item · annual seasonality per category · 14 named holidays
with per-item effects (Super Bowl pizza, Mother's Day cakes, Thanksgiving-eve bread) ·
weather (rain suppresses traffic, snow crushes it, the day *before* snow spikes bread) ·
temperature preferences (hot bar loves cold days) · payday bumps · a 2%/yr traffic
trend plus sushi growing ~12%/yr · plus irreducible noise and rare unpredictable
event days that no model should be able to learn.

## Known simplifications

- Everything is day-fresh: unsold units are wasted same day. Real cakes/bread carry
  multi-day shelf life (modeled here as lower cake noise instead).
- The status-quo "manager" is deliberately *not* a straw man: trailing same-weekday
  pars with a safety pad, corrections for chronic sellouts, a directionally-correct
  holiday instinct, and basic snow/rain awareness.
- Promotions/ads are not modeled yet (listed as an extension in the proposal).
